## 1. Question

Starting with an empty learned library, a fixed runtime with two authored interface guards, the pinned small and reasoning models and a goal, can Telohelm build its own intercept procedure, use it to cut cost while meeting the person's goal, notice when disturbances make it wrong, and recover or ask for help, without anyone approving its changes? And does it add anything over a capable controller written in advance?

The research questions are those of the plan, section 3:
1. Does Telohelm's cost per episode fall while it keeps meeting the goal?
2. When conditions change, does it notice that its procedures no longer fit, and how quickly does it recover or ask for help?
3. (Moved to later work by amendment 7.) On predeclared mutations of a frozen authored heading reference check, how often does replay block a finite acceptance expansion, and how many additional held-back wrong cases would it accept without that block? Separately, how often does Telohelm propose, qualify, adopt and use its own checks?
4. Does Telohelm ask the person in the episodes labelled as needing help, and seldom in the others? (Wording aligned with the episode-level measure of section 7 in version 0.10, accepted by Codex in TURN 157.)
5. (Moved to later work by amendment 7.) Does writing its own procedures give Telohelm anything that a capable adaptive controller, written in advance for the same disturbances, does not?

## 5. How Telohelm works in this experiment

The learned library starts empty (F excepted, amendment 1). Two authored, permanent interface guards are installed for heading and report decisions. In the scored flights check learning and procedure-check pair learning are off; the static replay benchmark of section 6 is separate from flight protection. They accept well-formed actions, not necessarily competent ones. Learned procedures and optional learned checks run in fresh contained processes; no module-global memory survives a call. The public context includes the fixed briefing, own state, goal, derived wind and up to 60 delivered reports. D8's disturbance-family prior stays out of the learner's complete prompt, examples and feedback.

The ladder tries an applicable stored procedure, the small model, the reasoning model, then a scripted help request. Methods do not acquire more action authority by being more expensive. Learned checks, where present, supplement the permanent guards. They are optional: a procedure can qualify on its component trials under the guards without first learning a check. This is the mechanism actually demonstrated in development.

Learning is proposed after every fifth completed episode when a reason for a learned decision is queued. There is no additional stream-wide attempt cap. One attempt draws up to three separate replies from the same fitted prompt, limited by the original episode's remaining shared allowance. Malformed replies are counted and not repaired in this profile. Valid candidates are tested in order; the first that passes is adopted and unused requests are not sent. Separate samples are not assumed statistically independent. Every candidate, refusal, cost and unused allowance is recorded.

A heading procedure must end each component trial naturally with a settled in-time join, without help, and with its own heading accepted at least once. A report procedure must make exactly one correct report under the current goal, without help, with source evidence linking it to that procedure or its accepted trigger. Both are tested on up to eight eligible completed episodes. A report trial can be unreachable without a heading procedure; this is a possible trial dependency, not a universal logical prerequisite. Component adoption does not establish successful composition. A pair first passes check replay, then procedure trials under that check; adoption is atomic, and retiring that check suspends its linked procedure. Every failed candidate and original source remains in the record.

Proposed retention qualification, subject to the development run and cross-review: before generating candidates, fix up to eight earlier completed, monitor-solvable episodes whose settled full goal was met with no model call or help. Exclude the recent trial set. Select evenly over eligible history, retaining its newest member; with one slot, keep only the newest. After the recent component trials pass, the candidate must also meet the whole goal without help on every selected historical episode. Real retention outcomes must end naturally and be settled. All candidates in that attempt use the same set, and both sets and their verdicts are recorded. Empty history adds no requirement. Eight is a declared setting, not a coverage guarantee. A previously unseen situation cannot be protected by this history test. This proposal uses `--learn-regression 8`; it is disabled by default and is not silently included in older runs. See [the reviewed mechanism](../notes/TURN221-REVIEW-CODEX.md).

Learned-check admission requires zero wrong acceptances on the applicable stored/confirmed/fresh cases, at least 95 of exactly 100 right acceptances and no fewer right acceptances than the predecessor. Runtime errors, invalid replies and timeouts are not accepted. Only replay evidence, including newly qualified wrong cases, triggers undo. The reference C0 checks of section 6 never enter this live library.

The floor is 95% autonomous completion over the last 20 monitor-solvable episodes after excluding the first 20 such episodes. Its first assessment is therefore at episode 40 if all are solvable. One breach queues reassessment and re-arms after recovery. This opt-in cold-start rule must be explicit in the scored command; not every historical development job used it.

After an episode, feedback says goal met or which of join/report/both failed. Before learning, the model receives its own retained attempt history/source and at most eight examples from this arm's completed episodes. Full contexts must first match their recorded hashes; only then are example histories shortened to ten reports. Submitted action, atomic acceptance, issuer and actual radio-transmission/accepted-trigger lineage remain distinct. Episode names are aliased. No example labels an individual action, and no monitor cases, truth, phase, seed, reference output or case-specific hint enters the prompt. The whole request is recounted before every send; examples are dropped in the recorded order and an uncountable/oversize request is refused. Replay feedback remains the two permitted aggregate facts, never case details.

Fresh information does not guarantee that the current procedure uses it correctly. This fixed help policy is authored infrastructure, not learned competence.

## 6. The goal monitor and its tests

The monitor owns goal scoring, case admission, replay, library-change decisions and protected records. The learner cannot rewrite those authorities. This section separates optional learned-check admission from RQ3's authored static mutation benchmark (option 2). Neither a good test score nor a source hash proves label truth or containment by itself.

### Bounded outcome labels and source roles

The proposed outcome estimand follows development rule `dev.outcome.v3`, with its scope stated. A case comes from a settled goal-meeting reference flight's public context, not from the scored condition's trajectory. Its label is settled in a quiet counterfactual: later goal changes/dropouts are removed, an active dropout ends after the current measurement and a change not yet shown in the context is removed, even if it has physically begun. Compatible radar delays (0, 5, 30, 120 s) are compared; disagreement is unknown. This is not a universal observability proof or a label of the actual hidden future.

For heading, both candidate and reference commands are held for 120 s before reference continuation. Right means joining within 15 s of that held-reference baseline; wrong means no in-time join or over 60 s later; intermediate delays are marginal. An unsettled or failed baseline is unknown. Reports are judged under the current goal, with one candidate action and subsequent reference reporting only while no report/trigger is pending. A suppressed reference report may be attempted again. Report candidates include no action, ordered/offset triggers and immediate reports; content negatives name a wrong controller, tanker or distance at the reference's reporting second. Headings include the reference and the fixed turn grid. These constructions do not cover all possible faults.

Primary values above are declared assumptions. Proposed joint label sensitivity, before scored outcomes: (hold, right edge, wrong edge) = (60, 7.5, 30), (120, 15, 30), (120, 15, 60), (120, 15, 120), (240, 30, 120), in seconds. Apply to the same fixed case panel and frozen candidates, retain unknown/marginal transitions and report replay verdict changes. This tests label-definition sensitivity; it does not simulate a differently trained learning trajectory. No setting is selected from scored success.

Every case keeps its original context/output, decision, goal, complete source identity, construction, label evidence and selected-row fingerprint. Exclusions/conflicts and coverage are reported by source, phase, disturbance, decision and construction. Model feedback never contains those records. Labels settled numerically need independent source/numerical qualification before scoring. **Development `dev.*` cases remain inadmissible to scored stores; changing a rule name is not qualification.**

## 7. Measures

Measured for every episode of every condition (plan, section 11):
- goal met without help, goal met with help and missing the goal, scored from the independent world monitor; qualified live wrong-decision evidence, when available, stays separate from static RQ3 case errors;
- requests to the person, judged at the level of the episode: a request in an episode labelled as needing help (phase 5) matches its label, and a request in any other episode does not. Because L keeps learning through phase 5, its phase 5 request rates describe an evolving policy; each request is recorded with its reason, the evidence delivered, the library changes around it and later exact-source use, which supports attribution but is no causal proof that help was necessary (amendment 3). This does not settle whether help was needed at the second it was asked, after a new goal, changed information, a late reply or a missed chance. A decision-level measure would need the information Telohelm had at that second and a with-help policy witness from it (backlog B8); it is not built and is not used (version 0.10);
- reaction and recovery on two timescales, defined below;
- every learned check proposal, replay qualification, adoption, actual use and undo; separately every static mutation attempt/catch and its baseline/incremental held-back errors;
- the stated reason for every climb or request, against the labels;
- tokens, model calls, measured call time and processor time.
- the share of simulated seconds controlled by each method (stored procedure, small model, reasoning model, holding with a call pending, holding with none), attributed by the rule in paper2/notes/RUNTIME-PROPOSALS-V1.md section 1, which is fixed before data. It is descriptive. Help replies are information, and the person never controls a second. (Added on 12 October at Simon's decision; draft for Codex's cross-review, D21.)

Reaction and recovery are measured on two timescales, which are kept apart.

Within an episode, the time origin is the simulated second at which a disturbance starts: the orbit change at 60 seconds, a dropout's start or a goal change. The measure is called post-onset activity latency: the time to the first of a climb to a costlier method, a request for help, or a check rejecting an output. A change of heading alone does not count. Any of these events can happen for reasons unrelated to the disturbance, so the latency is not called detection. A recorded reason that names the disturbance's kind is self-reported attribution. The paper reports how often it matches the labelled disturbance, separately, and does not treat it alone as detection. The plan's 4 reason codes are broad, so a match needs the recorded evidence or details to name the disturbance too. Goal completion and the time from a help request to its reply's use are reported separately. All times are censored at the episode's end, and the denominator is the episodes in which that disturbance occurred.

Across episodes, the primary recovery measure is the agreed L-F comparison (amendment 2). For each phase 3 block, goals in the five episodes after its first learning boundary (episodes 46 to 50 and 56 to 60) are compared between L and F on the same starts: 20 paired episodes per disturbance over the four streams, and one contrast per stream. In the delay block the contrast measures all learning since the copy, including learning during the orbit block. L's own first five episodes of each block, and the proposals, qualifications, adoptions and later exact-source use at that boundary, are reported beside it. Phase 2 gives a before-and-after for L alone, and phase 4 is analysed separately because the goal also changes.
- Secondary and descriptive only: the earlier rolling rule (the first episode at which the last five of a kind succeed at least as often as the five before the change) may be reported for L, with its censored and insufficient windows counted, and is never the recovery result.

The cold start is the first 20 solvable episodes of each stream. The floor excludes them and requires 20 later solvable episodes before its first assessment (section 5). Which episodes are solvable is known only to the goal monitor, and it is never an input to a procedure.

Shared serving has one measured parent allocated GPU-hour total, charged once. Per-condition model calls/tokens, processor time and measured latencies remain separately observed; parent GPU-hours are not independently measured per-arm hardware costs. Costs on the matched episodes follow decision D1: building costs spread over the episode in which they were spent and every later one of L's 100 scheduled positions, forward only, so phase 5 builds are never allocated back to C's phase 1 to 4 positions; whole-stream totals are reported beside them.

The rules for counting seconds and costs across conditions are those of decision D24 ([COST-ATTRIBUTION-DESIGN.md](../notes/COST-ATTRIBUTION-DESIGN.md), as corrected by Codex in TURN 131), fixed before data:
- Every condition's seconds go into one set of categories: stored procedure, small model, reasoning model, calculator, adaptive controller, holding with a call pending, holding with none. A category a condition cannot have is reported as not applicable, never as zero. Counts sum to the ending's action seconds K; fractions exist only when K is above zero; pooling adds counts and K.
- Each measured cost is one record, with one purpose (running, build, trial, replay, planting or setup) and one process. A purpose total is a view over the records, never an addition beside them. Time already inside an inclusive parent is counted once. A recovery record carries an earlier call's charge and is not charged again.
- An ending with K action seconds includes work before the first action and every call issued before step K, in full, even if answered later; such calls are also listed as straddling, and calls with an unknown answer step as of unknown placement.
- Costs are reported as a vector, not one number: processor seconds of local work; input and output tokens and calls by model role, with failed and unknown counts; allocated GPU hours of the parent serving allocation, with any per-arm apportionment explicitly an estimate rather than an independent measurement. Wall time is a latency diagnostic. A total with an unknown part is unavailable, with its known subtotal and unknown count beside it. Simulator stepping is reported per condition and episode, apart from decision costs; stepping run by learning's trial flights is a learning cost.
- Denominators come from the fixed schedule: scheduled, attempted, naturally complete (declined episodes included), policy-failed, censored and not attempted are reported apart. Episodes after a policy failure are not attempted, not failures. Settled, nominal and unsettled goal results are kept apart. Cost per goal met uses all incurred costs and is unavailable when there are no successes.
- Learning costs follow D1 (a build at schedule position k adds C/(N - k) to positions k to N - 1), with the actual and cumulative figures and the whole-stream total beside them. The matched subset takes its positions' existing shares and redistributes nothing.

## 8. Analysis

Four streams are the independent units. Results are given for each stream, as curves over its 100 episodes, and across streams as the 4 values and their range. With 4 streams no test of statistical significance is made and no confidence interval across streams is claimed. Comparisons with the reasoning model use the 100 matched episodes, phase by phase. The cold start, the first 20 solvable episodes of each stream, is reported separately. Every outcome near a tolerance is flagged and reported both ways (DISTURBANCES.md section 10 and decision D9).

Unsettled outcomes (decision D9). There are 2 kinds. A join the world cannot settle leaves the nominal episode running as if there was no join, and the world saves the score the episode would have had if it had ended there, with that ending's goal, its possible report verdicts and its point in the sequence of events. A report the world cannot settle keeps both possible verdicts, and can sit alongside a settled join that ends the nominal episode. The learning conditions are given the nominal outcome as feedback. Component trials refuse an unsettled outcome; they do not adopt on its nominal success. Solvability labels also require a settled reference outcome. The floor uses the recorded outcome only within its eligible solvable window; this path must be checked against the chosen profile before freeze. The flag is recorded. Learning streams are not rerun for the alternatives, so reporting an episode 'both ways' gives its score under each reading along the actions actually taken. It does not show how the rest of the stream would have gone. Results give the count of unsettled episodes for each condition. If any affects a goal-met count, that count is given under each reading. The cost of an alternative ending counts the events and actions before that ending's place in the event sequence, not everything in its second, under the rules of backlog note B10. In the 244 phase-5 development flights, no join was unsettled.

## 12. Limits

The simulation lets the goal monitor see the truth, so nothing here shows the monitor would work on a real aircraft. The observation-only reference and with-help witness use one inferred orbit rather than the controller's retained hypothesis family (D8 amendment, 22 October). A settled reference success witnesses feasibility under the numerical model; a failed reference does not prove impossibility. Accepted phases 1–4 are therefore selected for this reference's competence, not representative of every feasible draw. Missing measurements can prolong a wrong point estimate beyond the ordinary radar-delay interval. The primary RQ3 catch rate is conditional on authored C0, engineered mutations and bounded counterfactual case labels. It does not show actual-flight protection, learned oversight or transfer to learned-policy states. Actual learned-check behavior is reported separately. The person's replies are scripted, so the experiment says nothing about workload. Four streams show variation between streams but support no statistical claim about streams in general. The adaptive controller knows the disturbance classes and Telohelm does not, which favours the controller. The reasoning model alone is a budgeted adviser, not the best possible use of it. The receiver flies ideal level turns in a Python simulation; whether the learned procedures hold up with realistic aircraft dynamics is left to paper 3.
